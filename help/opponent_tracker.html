Opponent Tracker

The Opponent Tracker is a floating window opened from the Tools menu. It detects your current opponent’s username from the MTGO window title and looks up which archetypes they most commonly play.

Opening the tracker

Choose Tools ▸ Opponent Tracker on the menu bar. The window can remain open throughout your MTGO session.

How detection works

The tracker scans the titles of the windows open on your desktop for one containing vs. — which is how MTGO writes a match — and takes the name after it. No MTGO SDK or API key is required, and nothing is read from MTGO itself; the window title is all of it.

Results

Once an opponent is detected, the tracker shows:

  • The opponent’s username.
  • The most recent deck that player was seen on in each format, read from their MTGGoldfish player page.
  • A compact sideboard guide preview if you have a guide entry for any of their likely archetypes.
  • A radar chart of the most common cards in one of those archetypes — the first format with a known deck, not a summary across all of them.

Folding sections away

Each section — the hypergeometric calculator, the radar and the sideboard guide — has a ▼ caret in its heading. Click it to fold the section down to its heading; the space goes to the sections still open. Click ▶ to open it again. Folded sections stay folded the next time you open the tracker.

Compact mode

Compact in the tracker’s header swaps the window for a small floating icon showing the opponent archetype’s initials. Hover the icon to see the opponent’s decks, the radar’s top cards and your sideboard plan; click it to bring the full tracker back.

  • The first time, the icon starts roughly under the opponent’s portrait in the MTGO match window. MTGO doesn’t report where it draws the portrait, so this is an estimate.
  • Click the padlock to unlock the icon and drag it wherever you like; click it again to lock it in place so it can’t be moved by accident. It reopens where you left it.
  • The icon never takes focus away from MTGO.

Limitations

  • Detection only works while MTGO is running and a game is active.
  • Results depend on public tournament data; players who rarely enter events may show no data.

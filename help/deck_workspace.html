Deck Workspace

The Deck Workspace is the tabbed area in the centre of the main window. It shows the cards and supporting information for the currently loaded deck.

Tabs

TabDescription
Deck Tables The cards themselves. Mainboard and sideboard are shown at once, mainboard on top and sideboard below, split by a bar you can drag to give either zone more room; dragging a card across the bar moves it between the zones. Each zone can be shown as a Grid of card images, a sortable Table with Qty, Mana, Name, Type and Text columns, or Piles grouped by mana value, colour or card type — the choice is remembered per zone between sessions. The +, − and × controls add a copy, take one out, and drop the card entirely: they sit at the end of every row in the table view, and in the grid view they appear on a card when you hover over it or select it on its own. The mainboard header also carries the Art dropdown, which re-picks which printing is used for every card in the deck.
Sideboard Guide Matchup-by-matchup sideboarding notes. See Sideboard Guide.
Deck Notes Free-form text notes saved per deck. See Deck Notes.
Goldfish Shuffles the mainboard and deals opening hands so you can see what this deck actually draws. Opening the tab deals the first hand for you. New hand deals a fresh seven, Mulligan deals another and counts how many you would put on the bottom, and Draw takes the top card. Click a card in hand to put it on the table, or drag it where you want it; click a card on the table to tap it, and drag it back down onto the hand (or right-click it) to take it back. There are no game rules behind it — any card can be played at any time.
Deck Stats What the loaded deck is made of, as four charts: Mana Curve, Color Share, Card Types, and Lands in Opening Hand — the chance of drawing each number of lands in an opening seven, with six and up collapsed into one bar. A line above the charts counts the mainboard and the sideboard, how many cards in each are unique, and how many lands you are running, counting modal double-faced lands separately. Cards the card database cannot describe are counted out loud rather than folded into a card type.
History Every saved version of this deck, the branches between them, and what changed from one to the next. Saving a deck records a version, so this fills itself. See Deck History.
Baseline What this deck’s archetype always runs, and how many slots are actually yours: the cards every cached list of the archetype plays, and the ranked candidates for everything left over. See Archetype Baseline.

The Versions rail

The narrow column headed Versions between the deck tables and the Card Inspector lists this deck’s saved versions, newest at the top, so they are reachable without leaving the cards. Clicking one loads it — which rewrites the deck file on disk and replaces what is on screen. See Deck History.

Card rows

Hovering over a card previews it in the Card Inspector panel, for as long as nothing is selected — once you click a card the inspector stays on it and hovering no longer moves it. Clicking a card selects it and updates the inspector with full detail including mana cost, type, and card image.

Double-clicking a card

Double-click a card — in the grid, table or pile view — to take one copy out of that zone. It is the mirror of double-clicking a search result in the Deck Builder, which puts one copy in.

While a Sideboard Guide is being recorded the same gesture moves a copy instead: double-click a mainboard card to send one copy to the sideboard, and a sideboard card to bring one copy back. That is the same thing dragging the card across does, which is what the recording walk stores as the matchup's plan.

Collection overlay

If you have loaded your MTGO collection (via File ▸ Load Collection), a line under the tabs names what was loaded and how many entries it holds, and in the Grid view each card’s copy count is coloured by what you own: green when you have every copy the deck asks for, amber when you have some of them, and red when you have none. Until a collection is loaded the count is drawn in the ordinary subdued colour. The table and pile views do not carry the colouring.
